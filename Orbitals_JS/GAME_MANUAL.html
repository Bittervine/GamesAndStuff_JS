Starting the game

The game loads into a title overlay with the Orbital Core title art, a short controls reminder, and the prompt Press Fire to Start. The game begins when the player presses a fire control, clicks/taps, or presses the gamepad fire button.

After assets load, the status text changes to Press Fire to start.. The title overlay fades out only after the game is loaded and started.

The current game is a live 3D arcade space-flight prototype. It has planets, fuel motes, enemies, mothership invasions, score, crashes, mission messages, and HUD markers. It does not yet have shield pickups, rapid-fire pickups, or weapon upgrades.

Touch

Touch input is present but simpler. Touching the canvas starts the game and aims at the touched screen point. A normal touch fires. A quick double tap, within about 320 milliseconds, acts as boost.

HUD and screen symbols

The visible HUD is intentionally lean. It currently consists of the status panel, the reticle, a nose marker, mission messages, enemy markers, and the game-over overlay.

Enemy markers in detail

The game sorts enemies by HUD priority first and distance second, then shows the top 20. A marker is hidden only when there is no candidate. If an enemy is outside the central screen region, the marker is clamped toward the edge and shown slightly dimmer.

  • Normal markers are 16 by 16 pixels, red outline, transparent center, with a red glow.
  • Priority markers are 20 by 20 pixels, gold outline, transparent center, with a stronger gold glow.
  • Priority status is set by fields such as enemy.isPrimaryThreat or positive hudPriority.
  • Active presenter enemies use the configured presenter HUD priority and therefore appear gold.

Mission messages

Mission messages appear near the top center of the screen. Active messages use pale blue-white text. Success messages shift toward green/mint. Failure messages shift toward red/pink.

Game over overlay

When the player ship is destroyed, a centered GAME OVER card appears with a restart timer. Once the crash delay has elapsed, pressing a fire control restarts.

Flight model

Atmospheric / bound flight

When the ship is close to a planet, it is bound to that planet. Atmospheric flight has terrain protection, lift, stall behavior, banked turning, altitude trim, and automatic leveling when the player is not forcing pitch.

In this mode, turning banks the ship. Pitching up for too long without boost can stall and settle back down.

Free flight

When the ship climbs beyond the planet escape altitude, it becomes free. Free flight uses nose-coupled motion: the ship continues to travel along its nose. Current passive drag is effectively disabled.

Free flight does not use the same atmospheric roll/bank behavior. Camera roll slowly aligns to the ship.

Capture and escape

The ship starts bound to a planet. It escapes into free flight above the configured escape altitude. It can recapture into a planet's atmosphere when close enough and not protected by the short recapture delay.

Boost, brake, and fuel

  • Fuel starts at 100.
  • Boost uses a short boost timer and consumes fuel while active.
  • Fuel recharges when not boosting.
  • Fuel motes add 4 fuel when collected.
  • Brake slows the ship, especially in free flight.

Crashes

The player can crash into terrain, the sun, or another collidable ship. A crash freezes the ship, spawns a crash explosion, clears player projectiles, and starts the game-over timer. Current player shields do not exist yet, so there is no shield absorption in this build.

World and resources

Planet cluster

The world generates a dense cluster of 6 to 9 planets from the configured planet files. Planets move in chaotic-looking orbits with wobble, spin, atmosphere radius, gravity radius, and individual surface gravity.

Star and debris

The scene has a central star, space debris effects, atmospheric glow, lighting, and fog. Flying into the sun destroys the player.

Fuel motes

Each planet currently gets 10 fuel motes. They orbit in bands around the planet, pulse in size, and alternate mint and blue coloring. Touching one restores fuel and moves the mote along its orbit so it can be collected again later.

Fuel motes are the only implemented collectible in the current game state.

Weapons and combat

Current player weapon

The current player weapon fires one blue projectile per shot. The projectile is spawned from the ship position and travels in the chosen fire direction with added ship velocity. The default fire cooldown is 0.22 seconds.

Projectiles live for 8 seconds, vanish when they hit a planet, and damage enemies on contact. Current projectile damage is 15. Regular enemies have 10 hit points, so one projectile hit destroys a regular fighter.

Homing assist

Projectiles have a narrow homing assist. They can acquire or retain a target within a small forward cone and within the configured homing range. The homing turn rate is limited, so shots bend rather than snapping instantly.

Score

Destroying an enemy with a projectile currently adds 100 score. Crashes and silent removals do not use the same score path.

What is not in the current weapon system

  • No shield pickup yet.
  • No rapid-fire pickup yet.
  • No ThoriumGap-style weapon families yet.
  • No DART, TWIN, FAN, ROCKET, or BEAM upgrade tiers yet.
  • No enemy projectile fire yet.

Enemies and motherships

Enemy ship families

The game includes many enemy model families, including Standard, Crosspanel, FlyingSaucer, DeltaWing, Pirate, Orca, Longwing, TwoHoop, TigerWing, LunarCourier, Hooper, ManraRay, PyramidLifter, and Nemesis. Motherships use the FlyingSaucer family.

Mothership invasions

Motherships spawn after a delay, with up to 2 active motherships. A mothership targets a planet, approaches a hold position around roughly 2 planet radii, reorients belly-down, and releases fighters.

Fighter behavior

Fighters are released from the mothership, dive toward the target planet, settle into the atmosphere, and enter swarm/patrol behavior. The current patrol system creates orbital target points around the planet with formation offsets, altitude wobble, and some separation steering.

The current game still releases fighters one at a time. The dense 100+ fighter swarm is only in the new plan, not in this build.

Collisions

Regular enemy-enemy collision damage is disabled. Fighter-to-mothership collisions are also ignored. Player collisions with enemies can destroy the player.

Encounters and missions

Encounter director

The encounter director is enabled and runs inside the simulation. It tracks active encounters, chooses which encounter matters to the player, assigns presenters, assigns objective attackers for scaffolded objective encounters, updates encounter outcomes, and displays mission messages.

Planet invasion

This is the primary implemented live encounter. When a mothership starts invading a planet or releases fighters, the director creates or activates a planet invasion encounter. The mission text is typically Mission: Clear invasion at Planet N.

The planet invasion is cleared when the mothership is done releasing fighters or is gone, at least one fighter was released, and no living non-mothership encounter fighters remain.

Presentation passes

The director selects a small number of active presenter enemies. The current maximum active presenters is 2. Presenters get gold HUD markers and try to create readable target opportunities.

PatternCurrent behavior
behindCatchupEnemy stages behind the player, catches up into the forward firing lane, then escapes.
sideCrossEnemy stages on one side, crosses through the player's forward view, then escapes.
headOnBreakawayEnemy approaches from ahead, commits, then breaks away. This exists, but still needs polish according to the current plan audit.

Scaffolded encounter types

The simulation has state support for other encounter types such as transport defense, free-space ambush, convoy escort, and boss support waves. These are not normal player-facing browser gameplay yet. Some tests and helper paths exist, but transport attackers do not yet perform fully real combat damage in the natural game loop.

Debug tools and current gaps

Debug object

Debug mode is currently enabled in config. The browser exposes window.__orbitals with helpers such as:

  • state - the live simulation state.
  • snapshot() - a readable snapshot of ship, camera, planet, and pointer-lock state.
  • getCombatEvents() / getCombatLog() - copies of event log data.
  • dumpCombatLog() - formatted combat log text.

Current notable limitations

  • Only 10 to 15 fighters are released per mothership. The requested crowded-sky 100+ fighter behavior is planned, not implemented.
  • Presentation passes are often far away in the current build.
  • The config value for active threats near the player exists, but current live enforcement is incomplete.
  • Route anchors are not fully implemented for encounters.
  • Transport defense and similar objective modes are scaffolds rather than complete normal gameplay loops.
  • There are no shield, rapid-fire, or weapon-upgrade pickups yet.
  • The current weapon is a single projectile pattern with homing assist, not a multi-family upgrade system.
