RANGER'S QUEST III / RANGER OF BRACKENWALD
CAMPAIGN AND WORLD CONTINUITY HANDOFF
=====================================

Purpose
-------
This document is a continuity handoff for a new agent taking over the Ranger of Brackenwald interactive campaign. Its purpose is not merely to list story titles, but to preserve what has actually happened in the world: important decisions, people, places, alliances, unresolved threats, supernatural facts, political consequences, infrastructure changes, promises, and the current state of unfinished stories.

Important source note
---------------------
This reconstruction is based on prior campaign conversations and campaign summaries. Where the surviving conversation evidence is incomplete or contradictory, that is explicitly marked rather than silently filled in. A future agent should preserve uncertainty instead of inventing a false resolution.

Geographic authority
--------------------
The canonical campaign map is authoritative for major place names, their spelling, and broad geographic relationships. Older story text and summaries may contain naming mistakes. Where a major geographic name conflicts with the map, use the map. Story-established local places that do not appear on the map may still exist; absence from the map does not make a hamlet, ford, mill, shrine, road, tower, or other small location non-canon.

The order below is primarily the order in which the stories were played in chat. The campaign has not established a precise in-world calendar, so chat chronology should not automatically be treated as a strict day-by-day historical calendar.

The campaign also contains story drafts and coding/demo stories that were authored but never actually played. Those are listed separately near the end and should NOT automatically be treated as campaign history.

1. FIXED PREMISE AND ESTABLISHED PLAYER CHARACTER
======================================================================

The player is the Ranger of Brackenwald, sworn to Duke Aldric of Brackenwald. The Ranger is responsible for protecting the lands of Elderwood, the Deep Marshes, the Riverlands, Oakenhurst, and the Gray Mountains.

The Ranger is skilled with sword, bow, tracking, stealth, and healing herbs. The Ranger has no magic. The world itself can contain rare supernatural beings and ancient forces, and the Ranger has by now personally encountered several genuine supernatural or mythical phenomena.

The Ranger's horse is Thorne. Thorne is a recurring, important companion and is alive. Thorne has survived danger more than once, including a shallow spear injury at Blackroot Ford.

The Ranger has dark brown hair and stubble.

Duke Aldric is the Ranger's liege. Across the campaign Aldric has generally supported lawful investigations, accepted prisoners and evidence, funded repairs, approved settlements and alliances, and used the Ranger as a trusted field representative. He remains alive and ruling Brackenwald.

A strong behavioral pattern has emerged in actual play. The Ranger usually prefers investigation, evidence, restraint, surrender, restitution, and lawful judgment when possible. He has repeatedly tried to give wrongdoers a path back toward honest service. He is nevertheless willing to use lethal force when he believes people are in immediate danger or enemies are committed to violence. The Ranger is not a pacifist, but mercy and proportion matter to him.

One important player-side lesson came after the Miller's Secret story: after bloodshed against deserters, the player explicitly reflected that deserters might have had legitimate reasons for leaving an army and that the Ranger perhaps should have learned more about them before assuming the worst. This is useful characterization for future moral situations, though it should not be turned into an invented in-world oath unless the player says so.

2. BROAD GEOGRAPHY AND WORLD FRAME
======================================================================

Brackenwald is the political center of the campaign and the seat of Duke Aldric.

The canonical campaign map establishes the following major named geography:
- Brackenwald near the center of the mapped lands.
- Elderwood to the west of Brackenwald.
- The Deep Marshes and Gallows Corner toward the southwest.
- Bitter Hollow south of Brackenwald.
- Auldwatch Tower and Freview in the southwest/south.
- The Riverlands to the south and southeast, including Ervers and Greenvale.
- Everhurst Forest, Ilsengard, and Stirwatch in the southeast.
- Northwatch east of Frostfall Lake.
- Frostfall Lake northeast/east of Brackenwald.
- The Gray Mountains across the north and northeast.
- Northcai at the far eastern side of the Gray Mountains.
- Northgard north of Brackenwald, with Dunhold and Northpeak farther northwest.
- Graywatch and Raven Bay in the west/northwest.

The map is not exhaustive at village or local-feature scale. Story-established places such as Oakenhurst, Wickerford, Brindle Hollow, Harthmere, Blackroot Ford, Harrowspire, individual mills, shrines, roads, and similar locations may be canonical even though they are not labeled on the map. A future agent should distinguish major map geography from smaller story locations, and should not invent a conflict merely because a local place is absent from the map.

The world is grounded medieval fantasy. Ordinary farmers, ferrymen, millers, smiths, soldiers, merchants, nobles, smugglers, deserters, and travelers are much more common than mythical beings. Elves, trolls, treants/forest beings, ghosts or ancient spirits, giants, fairies, and similar beings can exist, but should remain uncommon enough to feel consequential.

3.1 UNNAMED EARLY ARC: THE BROKEN TOWER / YSELLA / GREYHAVEN PLOT
Status: Chapter I completed; a later Chapter II opening was started and then left unresolved.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

This early story did not have one consistent formal title. It began as what looked like ordinary violence and banditry and gradually widened into espionage, internal sabotage, hostage-taking, and foreign interference.

The Ranger's investigation exposed a network associated with the Broken Tower sign. People tied to this phase included Crow, Pater, Halden, Merek, Rusk, and evidence pointing east toward House Varrick of Lornhelm. Papers and courier material were recovered. Several conspirators, including Crow, Pater, Halden, Merek, and Rusk, were captured. Duke Aldric assigned Marwyn to work on the espionage and internal-security side of the problem.

Ysella Varne became entangled in the conspiracy because her brother had been taken and used as leverage. The brother's exact name was never established. IMPORTANT: he must not be called Thorne. Thorne is the Ranger's horse. Ysella initially occupied an ambiguous position because she had cooperated under coercion, but the Ranger treated her as someone who could help expose the deeper plot rather than simply as a criminal.

The Ranger traveled with Ysella to recover her brother. Clues referred to "the weeping stones" and "the mill that doesn't grind." The trail eventually connected to an old downstream fishery and a more serious Greyhaven-backed operation. A ferryman named Edrin was encountered in this phase. The enemy had established a foothold around the fishery and had even trained or controlled a dangerous river beast/leviathan for use in intimidation or defense.

A broker named Marrow of Greyhaven emerged as a principal external figure behind the operation. The conspiracy's broader aim was not merely theft. It was to create internal fracture inside Brackenwald, establish leverage and footholds, and weaken the duchy quietly.

The Ranger rescued Ysella's brother alive. He was weak but ambulatory and was later taken to the Brackenwald infirmary. The Ranger also defeated and disarmed a Greyhaven duelist rather than simply killing him. Some Greyhaven agents were allowed or able to withdraw. This means Marrow survived and learned the Ranger's identity/name.

The Ranger returned openly to Brackenwald and reported the Greyhaven connection, Marrow, the trained river beast, the fishery foothold, and the attempt to fracture Brackenwald from within.

Final status after Chapter I:
- Duke Aldric was fully warned.
- Ysella was not charged as a traitor. She had materially assisted in exposing the plot.
- Ysella and her rescued brother were placed under Duke Aldric's direct protection, with inner-keep chambers and discreet guards.
- Ysella was expected to tell Aldric everything she had heard and knew.
- Marrow remained at large in or associated with Greyhaven.
- Greyhaven withdrew for the moment, but the strategic threat was not resolved.
- The Broken Tower network had been badly damaged but not necessarily annihilated.
- House Varrick of Lornhelm had been implicated by evidence, but the full reach of that connection was not resolved.
- Marwyn was tasked with pursuing the internal espionage problem.

A subsequent Chapter II was proposed around investigating collaborators, going toward Greyhaven, or strengthening Brackenwald's defenses.

One actual continuation opening was started: the Ranger rode from Duke Aldric with orders toward Greyhaven and stopped at an inn called The Willow's Rest. A local father reported that his daughter had disappeared after going to fetch water. Her ribbon had been found tied to stone at an old ruin, and there were reports of childlike crying. The player did not complete a choice from that opening in the surviving record. Therefore the missing child/old ruin problem is unresolved and should not be silently treated as completed.

3.2 THE RIVER'S QUIET TEETH
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

At Wickerford, apparently dangerous river conditions concealed a deliberate human wrecking operation.

The hidden truth was that human wreckers were using snag-lines and false lantern signals to force boats into trouble, then ransom survivors and hide captives. The principal criminals were Ketch Varr and Odran Mote, with at least one additional crewman. A fourth accomplice, later known only as "Gray Cloak," remained hidden.

Named local figures included Reeve Marta Elwick, Ferryman Jory Pike, Brother Halden, and Nessa Reed.

The Ranger investigated suspicious cord, skiff evidence, tracks, and the riverbank. He captured and bound Odran, subdued another man, confronted and bound Ketch, and pursued a runner. He recovered a captive alive through a combination of pressure and mercy rather than simply killing everyone involved.

The captive survived, although thin and sore, and was capable of giving testimony. Nessa Reed remained with her rescued partner, and Brother Halden helped care for him.

Ketch Varr, Odran Mote, and the other captured crewman were taken to Brackenwald for Duke Aldric's justice. The river-lure crew was broken. Marta Elwick forwarded ledger pages and an account of the crime to Brackenwald. Jory Pike resumed river crossings.

The unresolved piece is Gray Cloak. The Ranger found signs of a fourth person who moved carefully through root, mud, shallow water, and reeds, tore out or damaged ledger material, and escaped unseen. Gray Cloak's identity and larger affiliation were never established.

This is a good dormant thread for a future story, but Gray Cloak should not automatically be tied to Marrow, the Broken Crown, Gallows Corner smugglers, or any other network unless evidence is introduced.

Because the player did not actually proceed, this should be treated as an unused or dormant opening rather than an event that already happened in the world.

3.4 THE ASHEN HARVEST
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

This story in the Riverlands initially suggested a dangerous monster problem, but the truth was more complicated.

A troll had been wounded by an iron spearhead and further harmed by human escalation and poisoning associated with a farmer named Corwin Hale. Rather than simply hunt the troll down, the Ranger eventually treated and bound its injury.

Named figures included Marta Feld, Corwin Hale, and Old Brennar. Old Brennar was found alive with a severe ankle injury. The Ranger splinted the injury and got him safely back to Marta.

No one was killed. The troll survived and withdrew deeper toward the Gray Mountains.

Corwin was made to accept responsibility. He promised to mend fences, repay lost sheep, and speak honestly to Duke Aldric's steward.

Later continuity:
- Old Brennar survived but walked with a cane.
- The troll remained alive somewhere deeper in the mountains.
- Corwin's promised restitution and steward report were the main remaining administrative follow-up.
- The story established a strong precedent that "monster" does not automatically mean "villain."

3.5 THE LANTERNS BENEATH GRAYWATCH
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Graywatch appeared to hide sinister underground activity, but the true danger was structural rather than supernatural.

Displaced mountain folk led by former Brackenwald scout Captain Elira Vance had been digging for an old Duke-era war cache beneath Graywatch. Their excavation destabilized an engineered counterweight/foundation system supporting or protecting the structure.

Other named figures included Garron Pike, Tobin Hale, and Old Marta.

The Ranger investigated openly, crossed a trapped spear corridor, stabilized a failing beam, reached the machinery below, and worked with Elira to stop a dangerous counterweight failure.

Rather than trying to preserve a fundamentally compromised structure, the Ranger ultimately chose controlled destruction. The counterweight was released into planned fractures. Graywatch's lower works and foundations were ruined and the tower was later dismantled, but the hillside and important foothill road were saved from a larger collapse.

Salvaged material included crossbows, bolts, short swords, two intact powder kegs, a ledger, and an iron box. Some buried supplies were lost.

The missing shepherds associated with the alarm were eventually found alive. Garron survived but was wounded and left limping. No deaths or arrests were recorded.

Captain Elira Vance was provisionally reinstated to help oversee the dismantling and cleanup. Her role should be remembered as complicated but ultimately cooperative rather than simply criminal.

Old Marta's warning about danger beneath Graywatch proved justified.

Long-term consequence:
- Graywatch as a functioning intact structure no longer exists in the same form; the dangerous structure was dismantled.
- Brackenwald's engineers began surveying for other forgotten structures or buried systems.
- There may be more old military engineering or forgotten caches elsewhere.

IMPORTANT NAME WARNING:
A different story, Lady Ravenheart, also contains a man named Garron Pike. The original stories never explicitly establish them as the same person. Their roles are very different. Treat this as an accidental or at least unproven name reuse unless future canon deliberately connects them.

3.6 LADY RAVENHEART
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Lady Elira Ravenheart was accused amid rising tension in the Riverlands after she sheltered a poisoned elven emissary, Maelin Thrice-Born.

The Ranger entered Ravenheart under Duke Aldric's authority and learned that the simple accusation against Elira concealed a larger attempt to poison relations between human communities of the Riverlands and woodland elves.

Maelin had been struck by a refined black-reed-sap toxin, delivered by dart. The Ranger identified the poison and helped keep him alive.

Garron Pike, a local reeve in THIS story, had access to levy stores and was tied to staged unrest, accusations, and the toxin operation. His actions also served ambitions around land and local control. Evidence later showed that two handlers had harvested/refined black reed sap and that traders had encouraged the effort to sabotage a possible human-elf alliance.

Other named figures included Ser Joric, Torvald, Marta, and Caelir of the Greenwood Veil.

The Ranger detained Pike and had him sent toward Brackenwald for formal inquiry rather than dispensing summary justice. Pike warned that the affair did not end with him.

Maelin survived and recovered.

The Ranger then helped turn the crisis into a political opening. A pact was formed involving communities and interests in the Riverlands and the Greenwood Veil. Elira, Joric, Maelin, Caelir, and the Ranger were involved, and Duke Aldric later sealed the agreement.

The pact established:
- Shared marsh/forest patrols.
- Mutual warning concerning movement or threats from beyond the Gray Mountains.
- No woodland claim on human farmland.
- No human trespass beyond agreed paths.
- A framework for cooperation rather than mutual suspicion.

A first joint human/elven patrol was launched.

Final status:
- Lady Elira Ravenheart's reputation was substantially cleared.
- Maelin fully recovered and later returned to the Greenwood Veil.
- Caelir openly stood beside the alliance.
- Garron Pike faced charges.
- The traders or wider interests behind the anti-alliance effort were not fully identified.
- The Riverlands-Greenwood Veil pact is a lasting political fact and should remain part of the world unless later events change it.
- Warning signs from beyond the Gray Mountains remain a legitimate background concern.

3.7 THE BELL BENEATH STILLWATER
Status: Completed. Ten-turn short scenario.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

At Brindle Hollow and Stillwater Pond, villagers feared a bell that seemed to toll from beneath the water.

The true explanation was partly natural and partly criminal. An old cracked and poorly cast bell lay in or near the remains of an old chapel. Marsh gas/trapped breath and movement in silt could make it vibrate or sound. Osric Dane discovered how to stir the pond and deliberately exploited the phenomenon to frighten people away so he could secure valuable north-bank timber.

Named figures included Brother Halwen and Marta Feld.

The Ranger investigated rather than accepting the supernatural explanation at face value. He tracked the disturbance back to Osric and exposed the fraud. Osric eventually confessed or was confronted with enough evidence to force restitution.

Rather than mob justice, the resolution required public responsibility and labor. Osric was made to work toward repairing the damage he had caused.

At dawn the bell was recovered and ultimately displayed silently near the old chapel stones as a local marker rather than allowed to remain an object of terror.

The pond became quiet again. Duke Aldric approved the resolution.

Unresolved lore:
- The older history of the chapel and bell was not deeply explored.
- It remains uncertain why the bell was originally so flawed or what older omen stories may have grown around it.
These are atmospheric threads, not evidence of an active supernatural threat.

3.8 THE TOLL AT BLACKROOT FORD / BLACKROOT FORD TOLL
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

At Blackroot Ford, Sir Garron Vale had established an unlawful toll and private militia.

The situation was not simply greed. Garron believed Duke Aldric had failed to respond strongly enough to raids and caravan burnings, so he built his own defensive force and funded it through the toll.

Mirela Voss, a foreign manipulator/agent, exploited Garron's frustration. She gradually reframed local defense as defiance against Brackenwald and encouraged choices that would weaken Duke Aldric's authority. She had suspiciously been present around the earliest caravan burnings and fled before she could be properly questioned.

Ellyn Hart was a young, conflicted archer within Garron's force. Tomas Reed represented aggrieved villagers/travelers.

The Ranger's own handling was imperfect. During a confrontation he seized the strongbox and rode away, which temporarily damaged his credibility and made him appear to some observers like a thief rather than a lawful officer. Thorne also suffered a shallow spear wound. Some coin from the toll remained missing.

Nevertheless, Garron ultimately stood down rather than forcing a larger battle. He publicly dismantled the barricade, dismissed the militia, reopened the ford, returned recoverable money, and renewed his loyalty to Duke Aldric while asking for a formal inquiry into the original raids/burnings.

Ellyn left tollkeeping/the militia.

Mirela Voss escaped and remains at large.

Long-term consequences:
- Blackroot Ford is lawfully open.
- Garron Vale is a compromised but potentially redeemable figure who chose to return to lawful allegiance.
- Some victims may still resent missing toll money.
- The original caravan burnings deserve investigation.
- Mirela Voss remains one of the campaign's clearest unresolved human antagonists.
- Do NOT automatically connect Mirela to Greyhaven, Broken Crown, or other networks without new evidence.

3.9 THE WEEPING STONES OF ELDERWOOD
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Near Bracken Hollow, old stones in Elderwood seemed to "weep," and local fear grew around an ancient site.

The truth involved greed, archaeology, and a real ancient supernatural presence.

Roderic Vale had widened cracks and disturbed an old sealed chamber because he was searching for a kingship torque buried within. Clear water passing through disturbed red clay contributed to the physical "weeping" effect, but Roderic's intrusion also disturbed an oath-bound ancient warrior within the burial place.

Named local figures included Brother Halven, Marta of Bracken Hollow, and Old Garrun.

The Ranger descended into the chamber and treated the ancient warrior with respect rather than as a thing to destroy or loot. The Ranger swore to reseal and protect the resting place. The torque was NOT taken.

Roderic helped reseal the chamber and swore secrecy. The warrior accepted the Ranger's word and returned to rest.

The stones became quiet. Villagers returned to their fields. Brother Halven publicly framed the event in less alarming terms as a disturbed burial place.

Roderic was effectively banished under an oath of secrecy. He was not formally arrested or punished through Duke Aldric's courts. The epilogue indicated that he kept his oath and did not return, but the theoretical risk remains that he could break it.

Important lasting facts:
- The ancient warrior and chamber still exist.
- The torque remains buried.
- The oath/place should not be casually looted or reopened.
- The Ranger personally gave his word concerning the resting place.
- The story establishes that some old places in Elderwood genuinely contain ancient supernatural remnants.

3.11 THE CROWS OF DUNMERE HOLLOW
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Farmers in Dunmere Hollow were being driven from their land by strangely aggressive, organized crows.

The birds were trained. Osric Vale was using them, along with horn signals, to frighten landholders into abandoning or selling property cheaply. His real interest was an iron deposit hidden beneath or near Marta Fenlow's land.

Named figures included Marta Fenlow, Edrin Holt, Brother Caldus, a horn-boy, and guards.

The Ranger investigated the bird behavior and signal system, disabled the horn operation, captured the horn-boy and guards, and exposed documentary/testimonial evidence of Vale's scheme.

Osric Vale was arrested. There were no deaths.

His fraudulent contracts were voided, his holdings were seized or subjected to legal action, and fines/restitution were imposed. The horn-boy was not treated as an irredeemable villain; he was assigned restitution.

Marta stayed on her land. Edrin remained in the community.

Potential future issue:
The iron itself still exists. If anyone wants to extract it lawfully later, land rights, consent, safety, and Duke Aldric's authority should matter. The fact that Vale abused knowledge of the deposit does not make all future mining automatically illegitimate.

3.12 THE LANTERN IN THE REED MIST
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The Ranger uncovered a smuggling operation using marsh and ferry routes.

Lysa Halvick, the ferryman's daughter, was threatened by Garrick's ring. The Ranger chose careful reconnaissance and protected her.

Named criminals included Garrick and Pell the Hook. During the operation the Ranger used lethal force against several committed attackers, including Pell and Garrick, while preserving at least one surrendering smuggler as a live witness.

The Ranger recovered smuggled iron and Garrick's ledger. The ledger contained a broken-crown symbol and evidence that unknown buyers beyond the immediate ring were obtaining iron.

The captured smuggler was taken into custody and questioned. Other accomplices were arrested. The recovered iron was returned to Duke Aldric.

Lysa Halvick's ferry was restored, and she became a friendly/loyal contact of the Ranger.

The Ranger pursued evidence as far as a Stone Marker but deliberately chose patient investigation rather than charging across the border into unknown territory.

Major unresolved thread:
The "Broken Crown" buyers were not identified. They appear to have been gathering iron for an unknown purpose outside or beyond the immediate local smuggling ring. This should remain a separate unresolved conspiracy unless later evidence connects it to another faction.

3.13 THE MILLER'S SECRET AT GALLOWS CORNER
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

At an abandoned or troubled mill around Gallows Corner, the Ranger discovered that Tomas Feld had found a hidden cache of old weapons beneath the mill. Deserters led by Captain Erwald were coercing Tomas into opening or surrendering the cache.

Other named figures included Mira Feld and Old Harker.

The Ranger approached stealthily, investigated beneath the mill, found the concealed entrance/cache, and eventually armed himself with a crossbow from the old stores.

The confrontation became bloody. The Ranger killed outer guards/deserters during ambushes and fighting. He broke a ladder to control movement. He tackled Captain Erwald and was cut in the struggle. He later used lantern light/firelight tactically to disarm Erwald.

Erwald survived. The Ranger bound him and delivered him to Duke Aldric's justice.

Tomas survived unharmed. The old weapons cache was resealed. The remaining deserters scattered.

A friendly silver tabby cat appeared during this story in the campaign's recurring, unexplained helpful-cat motif.

This story has an important unresolved moral dimension. The deserters were coercing Tomas and intended to get old weapons, which made them a real immediate danger. However, the Ranger never fully learned WHY they had deserted or whether some had legitimate grievances. Afterward the player explicitly questioned whether the Ranger had been too quick to assume the deserters were simply bad men.

Captain Erwald is therefore a useful possible future source of information if he remains in custody. His motives and background were never fully explored.

Status: Opening definitely generated; completed outcome is NOT reliably documented.

The story opened in Mirefen in the Deep Marshes.

Named figures in the scenario were Tomas Reedwell, Elira Reedwell, Garrick Vane, Brother Halven, Old Mara Fen, and Lute Carrow.

The hidden scenario premise was mundane criminal deception: smugglers were using reed whistles and lantern signals to imitate marsh-spirit activity and frighten villagers away from a contraband landing site. The smugglers were led by a disgraced former Brackenwald soldier.

3.15 THE WHISPERING MILL OF OAKENHURST
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

A mill near Oakenhurst began to exhibit genuinely uncanny behavior: whispers, poisoned-looking water, and a wheel that could turn without ordinary cause.

The truth was supernatural, but not malicious haunting.

Fenrick's digging had severed or injured the dormant root-heart of an ancient being called Rootfather. An iron shard and black sap contaminated the water and aggravated the wound. Rootfather's pain manifested through the mill and water.

Named figures included Fenrick, Marta, the herbalist Lysa, and Rootfather. Sir Caldus was named in the scenario but was not central to the resolution. A silver tabby also appeared.

The Ranger did not attack Rootfather. He used healing knowledge, removed the harmful iron shard, applied herbs/bindings, and communicated/restored rather than escalating.

Rootfather healed enough to return to sleep.

The Ranger later rode to fetch Lysa, an herbalist living near the Elderwood edge, so Fenrick could properly learn how to care for the water and avoid repeating the damage. Fenrick effectively apprenticed under Lysa.

A seven-night herb watch helped clear the water.

A coffer, journal, and ring recovered during the story were returned to Duke Aldric. Their value benefited veterans' families rather than becoming private loot.

Long-term state:
- Rootfather is asleep and should be left undisturbed.
- Fenrick is expected to maintain responsible water stewardship.
- Lysa the herbalist is alive.
- The mill and water are stable.
- Continued quiet observation of the site is sensible.

IMPORTANT NAME WARNING:
This Lysa the herbalist is not established as the same person as Lysa Halvick the ferrywoman or the pregnant Lysa in the Midwife story. Treat the three as distinct unless future canon explicitly unifies them.

3.16 THE BROKEN CAUSEWAY OF FROSTFALL
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

At Frostfall Lake, a damaged causeway and rising water threatened travelers and potentially much of the Riverlands.

The core problem was deliberate sabotage. An old spillway had been blocked with a human-placed beam, raising the lake and undermining the causeway.

Mira Fenrick, a ferryman/skiff operator familiar with the lake's currents, assisted the Ranger. She had seen suspicious men lowering something by rope and helped the Ranger remove the beam.

The Ranger and Mira reopened the spillway, lowering and stabilizing the water and averting a larger flood.

Harl Vexley and Torren Bale were captured. Torren had arranged or profited from the sabotage because disruption to the causeway increased ferry/toll profits.

The investigation also uncovered parchments connected to a wider group of merchants involved in toll evasion or corrupt traffic.

Garrik Stonehand was given authority and funding to repair the causeway. Mira ferried travelers while repairs were underway.

Punishments focused on repair and lawful restitution:
- Torren forfeited assets toward repairs.
- Harl was sentenced to labor under Garrik.
- Other implicated merchants faced fines or scrutiny.

Trade recovered.

A silver tabby appeared during this story as part of the recurring motif.

Long-term state:
- The Frostfall causeway is restored/repaired.
- The spillway should remain clear.
- The immediate sabotage ring was broken.
- Wider merchant corruption/toll-evasion contacts may still exist, though no immediate local crisis remains.

3.17 THE MIDWIFE OF THE RIVERPEOPLE
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Meg, an elderly Riverpeople midwife, asked the Ranger for help after a young pregnant woman named Lysa disappeared.

The disappearance was not an abduction by a monster or supernatural force.

Lysa had fled because she believed Bran, the father of her child, had betrayed her to his father Osric. Osric wanted to suppress what he regarded as family shame. A ferryman named Edrin hid Lysa in an abandoned reed hut on a river islet to protect her.

The real immediate threat was social fear, pride, secrecy, and a rising spring river.

The Ranger tracked ferry clues, crossed quietly, found Lysa, defused conflict involving Bran and Osric, secured safe ferry access, summoned Meg, and helped the group through the dangerous timing of flood and labor.

Lysa gave birth safely to a daughter during the flood.

Mother and child were escorted to Meg's cottage.

No one died and no one was arrested.

Bran and Lysa reconciled.

Osric publicly accepted the child and promised the baby a name and home, turning what could have become an exclusion/shame story into a public family acknowledgment.

The village later blessed or welcomed the child.

Edrin's role in sheltering Lysa was protective, not criminal.

Long-term state:
- Lysa, Bran, their daughter, Meg, Osric, and Edrin survived.
- There is no major unresolved villain from this story.
- Ordinary practical support, ferries, and Riverpeople trust remain the main continuity.
- Meg is a useful Riverpeople contact.

3.18 THE BELL OF GALLOWS CORNER
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Strange bell activity around Gallows Corner was tied to a criminal river-crossing network.

The hidden old watch-bell cellar was being used by smugglers as a signal station for illegal crossings.

Corvin Hale led the smuggling organization. Tomas Reed had rung the bell seeking help after Hale's men killed his father. Other named figures included Marta Reed, Ellyn Voss, and Old Garrick.

The Ranger uncovered the signal network, freed a captive woman, disabled the smugglers' wagon/ferry logistics, fought and bound smugglers, and arrested Corvin Hale and Ellyn Voss.

Ledgers were seized. They showed a wider corruption network and contained a particularly important entry: "Reed farmer interfered. Removed." This directly confirmed the killing was part of organized criminal policy, not a random quarrel.

Tomas was protected from retaliation and escorted to Oakenhurst. He later went to Marta.

Hale and several smugglers were later convicted. Voss lost influence.

The bell itself was removed from the hidden criminal signaling role and relocated to the Oakenhurst watchtower.

Unresolved thread:

3.19 THE COUNCIL OF ELVES
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

This was one of the campaign's most important diplomatic and supernatural stories.

The Ranger became involved with Elderwood elves including Lady Sylvariel, Caelthorn, Miriel, and Thalanwe.

A human smuggler group led by Bren Torvik had removed an old warding stone near Oakenhurst. The smugglers did not merely steal a valuable object; by removing it they weakened an ancient seal around a dangerous entity known as the Thornbound Sentinel.

The elf faction initially had understandable reason to distrust humans. Thalanwe in particular was hostile or suspicious. The Ranger responded by offering cooperation, accepting that human wrongdoing had contributed to the problem, and promising the same restraint he would expect elves to show if investigating an elven wrongdoer.

Caelthorn became the Ranger's principal field partner. The two worked together rather than one side treating the other as subordinate.

The Ranger and Caelthorn recovered the warding stone from Bren Torvik's smugglers. The Ranger explicitly tried to capture rather than slaughter the smugglers, while allowing self-defense if necessary. Torvik and his men were taken alive.

The warding stone was restored. Old runes were renewed or prepared for renewal. The Thornbound Sentinel returned to dormancy.

Bren Torvik and his men were delivered to Duke Aldric for trial.

No deaths were recorded in the resolution.

Political consequences:
- Lady Sylvariel supported a cooperation agreement.
- Thalanwe's hostility cooled, though distrust did not vanish.
- Miriel remained important for ward-lore.
- Caelthorn and the Ranger became partners in a shared watch over the Elderwood seal.
- Duke Aldric approved the arrangement.
- Human-elven trust in Elderwood materially improved.

Supernatural consequences:
- The Thornbound Sentinel still exists.
- The seal still requires maintenance.
- The warding stone and runes must not be treated as ordinary loot.
- The Sentinel is a latent danger if the protections fail.

Immediately after the main crisis, a silver tabby led the Ranger toward a smuggler mark/cache sign in the Riverlands. The Ranger recognized it as potentially important. This suggests Torvik's smuggling network may extend beyond the captured group, but no full follow-up has yet resolved that clue.

3.20 THE LOCKED TOWER
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

This story centered on Harrowspire in the Gray Mountains.

3.22 THE BLACKWATER OATH
Status: Completed.
----------------------------------------------------------------------

At Blackwater Ford and the surrounding Deep Marshes, Ser Edric Vane had seized control, demanded tribute, and sent people into the marsh.

Named figures included Tovin Reed, Mara Fen, Old Brann, and Captain Halric.

Unlike several earlier "haunting" stories, the supernatural threat here was real.

Edric had entered into a pact with an ancient unseen Marsh Presence. The pact initially promised protection but became increasingly demanding. The tribute system eventually involved people and disappearances.

The Ranger investigated the old testimony, the pact stone, and rune-marked connection rather than simply attacking Edric.

Living captives were ultimately recovered.

The Ranger broke the coercive binding represented by the pact stone, but did not destroy the Presence itself.

In one of the campaign's more personal acts, the Ranger offered his own ring during parley. He later refused to simply take it back without permission, saying that the ring was more valuable to him than to the Presence and asking whether he could earn it back. This became part of the moral negotiation rather than a mere barter trick.

The final settlement rejected human sacrifice and forced tribute.

The new public terms were:
- No lives as tribute.
- The marsh and its boundaries must be respected.
- People should avoid needless harm in the deepest marsh.
- Memory and respect matter.
- Villagers may leave voluntary crafted offerings made of wood, reeds, or stone.
- The village itself consented to the new arrangement rather than having secret terms imposed by one frightened leader.
- Boundaries were marked and rules were taught openly so the pact could not again become a private source of power.

Mara Fen helped lead the open village decision and education.

Tovin Reed supported reconciliation but insisted there be no more secrets.

Ser Edric survived. He lost his rank and was subjected to judgment, but was not executed. His punishment became service/protection rather than death.

The Marsh Presence remained, weakened and no longer coercively bound in the old way. Its final attitude was essentially that it was "bound no more" but would not be forgotten.

The Ranger's ring was returned after the Ranger promised to carry the truth of the marsh and remember what had happened.

Old Brann and Captain Halric survived as far as the preserved record shows, but their exact final duties/status were not stated.

IMPORTANT META-CONTINUITY NOTE:
After the story, the player criticized the scenario's original planning because the Marsh Presence had been too vaguely explained behind the scenes. The game play itself was acceptable, but the hidden truth felt handwavy. For future continuity, do NOT "solve" that by casually inventing a grand cosmology for the Presence. Its exact nature remains underdefined in canon. If it returns, give it a coherent explanation based on evidence developed in that new story rather than retroactively asserting unsupported lore.

13. GUIDANCE FOR A NEW STORY AGENT
======================================================================

Preserve consequences. Do not reset the world to generic medieval fantasy each time.

When using geography:
Use the canonical campaign map for major place names, spelling, and broad spatial relationships. Use this handoff for what has happened at those places. Smaller story locations can be canonical without appearing on the map.

When using Elderwood:
Remember that the Ranger now has real history with elves, Caelthorn, warding stones, the Thornbound Sentinel, the Weeping Stones burial site, Rootfather, and the unfinished Briar Crown crisis. Do not casually pile all of these together into one ancient-supernatural conspiracy. They are currently separate pieces of history.

When using the Riverlands:
Remember the Greenwood Veil pact, the Riverpeople relationship, restored crossings, the Gallows Corner corruption case, and multiple ferries/causeways whose status has changed.

When using the Gray Mountains:
Remember the Ashen Harvest troll, dismantled Graywatch, Harrowspire, and the newest Longscar Mine crisis.

When using smugglers:
There are already several independent smuggling/criminal threads. Resist the temptation to reveal that every smuggler in the duchy secretly works for one master organization unless the player actually uncovers convincing evidence.

When using old ruins or supernatural forces:
The user has explicitly objected to vague "there is a mysterious Presence because the plot needs one" planning. The hidden truth should be coherent from the beginning. If a supernatural being matters, decide what it is, what it wants, what its limits are, why it acts now, and how the clues fit.

When writing moral conflict:
The Ranger has repeatedly rewarded surrender, restitution, and useful service, but he has also killed in combat. Moral uncertainty works well when there is a real reason to investigate rather than a fake binary between "obviously good" and "obviously evil."

When using recurring characters:
Bring people back when their existing role makes sense. Caelthorn, Lysa Halvick, Meg, Cadan, Ysella Varne, Elira Ravenheart, Mira Fenrick, Captain Elira Vance, or others can make the world feel persistent. Do not force cameos merely to prove continuity.

When using the silver tabby:
Keep it rare and unexplained.

14. COMPACT HANDOFF SUMMARY
======================================================================

If only a few paragraphs can be retained, preserve this:

The Ranger of Brackenwald is Duke Aldric's non-magical ranger, traveling with his horse Thorne. He has become known for investigation, restraint, lawful arrests, restitution, and mercy where possible, though he has killed when he judged it necessary. Over many adventures he has broken wrecking and smuggling rings, restored roads and crossings, saved villagers and travelers, negotiated with elves, treated a wounded troll, respected an ancient warrior's burial chamber, healed the forest-being Rootfather, restored the ward imprisoning the Thornbound Sentinel, and renegotiated a deadly marsh pact so it no longer accepts human tribute.

Duke Aldric remains secure. Human-elf relations are significantly improved: communities of the Riverlands have a pact with the Greenwood Veil, and the Ranger shares watch of an Elderwood seal with the elf Caelthorn. Blackroot Ford and Frostfall causeway are restored; Graywatch was dismantled for safety; Harrowspire resumed proper duty. Several criminals were captured and sometimes redirected into service or restitution rather than execution.

Major unresolved human threats include Marrow of Greyhaven, foreign manipulator Mirela Voss, the unidentified Gray Cloak, Broken Crown iron buyers, a wider Gallows Corner corruption network, and possible surviving branches of Bren Torvik's smuggling network. Captain Erwald survived the mill/deserter fight, and his deeper motives were never explored.

Major supernatural continuity includes the sealed Weeping Stones warrior and buried torque, sleeping Rootfather, sleeping Thornbound Sentinel behind maintained wards, the still-existing Blackwater Marsh Presence under a new no-sacrifice agreement, and the living briar crown in the unfinished Briar Crown story. These should not be merged into one magical system without evidence.

Two unfinished/current story states matter. In The Briar Crown of Elderwood, play stopped at Turn 11 with Lady Ysabel Vane holding the living crown at a broken shrine while briars move around her; the Ranger is hidden nearby and Toman Reed waits with Thorne. In the newest scenario, The Rustwater of Harthmere, Turn 1 has been presented but no action chosen: illegal reopening of Longscar Mine caused acidic metal-contaminated orange water, a concealed bulkhead may fail, villagers have blockaded the mine road, and Rorik Dane is lying about the cause.

Finally, several authored story files and unused openings are NOT automatically canon. The False Fires of Saint Werran and The Ashwater Crossing were project/story artifacts, not documented played outcomes. The 1000 Year Acorn and Frost Beneath Elderwood never progressed beyond their opening turn. The Whispering Reed has an established hidden setup but an uncertain actual ending. Preserve those distinctions.
